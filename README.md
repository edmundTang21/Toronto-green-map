# 🌿 Toronto Green Map

**An interactive map exploring Toronto's green spaces, urban trees, parking, flooding, and walkability — all in one place.**

🔗 **Live at [edmund-tang.us](https://edmund-tang.us/map/)**

![Map screenshot](screenshot.png)

---

## 🗺️ What the Map Shows

Toronto Green Map layers real city data onto an interactive map so you can explore how the city's built and natural environments overlap:

| Layer | What it means |
|-------|---------------|
| **Urban trees** | Every street tree the city tracks — see where the canopy is dense or sparse |
| **Green spaces** | Parks, ravines, and natural areas across the city |
| **Green Streets** | Roads with planted medians, bioswales, or other green infrastructure |
| **Impermeable surfaces** | Rooftops, pavement, and concrete that can't absorb rain — a key driver of flooding |
| **Flood-prone areas** | Neighbourhoods identified as at higher risk during heavy rainfall |
| **Parking lots** | The footprint of surface parking across the city — often large, paved, and flood-worsening |
| **Population density** | Where people live, to put everything else in context |
| **Sewer inlets & rain gauges** | Infrastructure that manages stormwater runoff |
| **Elevation contours** | The gentle hills and valleys that shape how water (and walkability) flow |

---

## ✅ What You Can Do

- **Toggle any layer on or off** — mix and match to explore relationships (e.g. parking + flood risk)
- **Switch basemap styles** — choose between satellite, street map, or minimal views
- **Use the Walk Isochrone tool** — click anywhere on the map to see what's reachable on foot within 5, 10, or 15 minutes. Great for understanding walkability in any neighbourhood.

---

## 📂 Data Sources

All data comes from publicly available civic datasets:

- **City of Toronto Open Data** — trees, green spaces, parking, flood susceptibility, sewer infrastructure, land cover, and more
- **Toronto Parking Authority (Green P)** — parking facility locations
- **Statistics Canada (2021 Census)** — population density
- **Toronto and Region Conservation Authority (TRCA)** — rain gauge monitoring stations
- **Natural Resources Canada** — elevation data

---

## 💡 Why This Exists

Toronto — like most North American cities — has traded a lot of green space for pavement. Surface parking lots alone cover enormous stretches of the city. That pavement heats neighbourhoods in summer, speeds up stormwater runoff, and contributes to localized flooding when it rains hard.

At the same time, the city's urban forest, ravine system, and green streets are valuable assets that many residents never fully see or appreciate.

This project brings those layers together in one place to make the trade-offs visible — for residents, planners, journalists, students, or anyone curious about the city they live in.
